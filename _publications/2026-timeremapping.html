Yet this capability as a design consideration remains underexplored. We define and formalize time remapping as the intentional, real-time manipulation of how visual world time is presented to the user, and model it as a first-order dynamical system that balances delay accumulation and recovery. To evaluate its perceptual effectiveness, we conducted a within-subjects study (N=21) in which participants identified briefly visible playing cards under baseline and remapped conditions, varying temporal anchor (self vs. external) and control (user vs. system). Time remapping approximately doubled identification accuracy without significantly reducing comfort, with gains consistent across all remapped conditions. Building on these findings and qualitative interviews, we derive a six-dimensional design space and instantiate it through three application prototypes. This paper establishes time remapping as a practical design material, introducing programmable time as a new dimension for AR experience design.
